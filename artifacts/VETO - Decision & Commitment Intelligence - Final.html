01

Executive summary — what this use case is

VETO is a proactive, governed decision-intelligence copilot for Executive Assistants and Chiefs of Staff. It converts fragmented Microsoft 365 signals into a ranked, evidence-backed decision brief, tests proposed commitments, recommends feasible calendar options, and learns safely from EA corrections.

VETO has two complementary operating modes built on the same intelligence layer. First, it works proactively: every morning, or on another approved cadence, it reviews a bounded set of recent communications and calendar context and sends a concise Decision Brief. Second, it works on demand: before a leader commits to a date, request, priority, or action, the EA can ask VETO to check the commitment against source evidence, known commitments, and hard calendar constraints.

The product does not replace the EA or executive. AI performs language-heavy work such as extraction, evidence linking, contradiction detection, and explanation. Deterministic services enforce permissions, priority rules, scheduling feasibility, approval requirements, and allowed actions. The EA remains responsible for judgment and approval.

Release 1 has one primary operating persona: the Executive Assistant / Chief of Staff. The Executive / Senior Leader is the beneficiary and decision owner, receiving a concise view of the same governed decision case. Project and Program Managers remain a later expansion after the Microsoft 365 decision loop and its recursive improvement controls are proven.

Use case in one sentence

VETO helps an EA protect executive time by turning scattered meeting and commitment signals into evidence-backed options, flagging conflicts, and creating an unsent calendar draft only after explicit EA approval.

Product question Release 1 answer
What problem are we solving? Too much fragmented communication and too little decision-ready context.
What is the differentiator? Not another digest: VETO checks commitments before they are made and improves from EA corrections through a governed, reversible loop.
Is it autonomous? Yes for bounded retrieval, synthesis, prioritization, recommendation, and candidate improvement generation; no for consequential actions or behavior changes without approval.
Who is the MVP for? Primary: Executive Assistant / Chief of Staff. Beneficiary and decision owner: Executive / Senior Leader.
What data is minimum? Outlook + Teams + Calendar + Dataverse decision/commitment ledger. SharePoint is useful; ADO is Next.

Microsoft’s 2025 “Infinite Workday” telemetry describes a high-volume work environment: the average worker receives 117 emails and 153 Teams messages a day, and Microsoft 365 users are interrupted roughly every two minutes. More than half of leaders surveyed said their work feels chaotic and fragmented. [1]

The coordination problem is not only message volume. Microsoft’s 2023 Work Trend Index found that 62% of respondents struggled with too much time spent searching for information, 68% said they lacked enough uninterrupted focus time, and the average Microsoft 365 user spent 57% of time communicating rather than creating. [2] Atlassian’s 2025 State of Teams found leaders and knowledge workers waste 25% of their time searching for answers. [3]

Meeting load is also material for the target audience. Atlassian found 78% of surveyed workers said the volume of meetings makes it hard to get work done; the reported overtime rate due to meeting overload rose to 67% for director-level and above. [4]

These data points justify the problem space, but they do not by themselves prove VETO’s exact feature set. The persona-specific needs, ranking logic, and willingness to receive proactive briefs should be validated through interviews and pilot telemetry.

Product-management interpretation

The opportunity is not “summarize my inbox.” The higher-value problem is “tell me what requires a decision, assemble the evidence, expose trade-offs, and help me protect attention.”

03

Current state, problem statement, and pain points

Today, the user is the integration layer between email, chat, meetings, documents, calendars, and work systems.

Important requests arrive through different channels and at different times. The recipient must decide whether something is informational, urgent, a commitment, a risk, a meeting request, or a decision that needs broader context. That interpretation is often repeated by the executive, EA, PM, and other stakeholders.

Even when AI summarizes a thread, the user still has to answer the harder questions: What changed? Is this important? What is the deadline? What am I already committed to? Who is blocked? Do I need a meeting? Can I move something else? What evidence supports the recommendation?

Problem statement

Leaders and the people who support them lack a permission-aware, evidence-backed way to continuously convert fragmented work signals into a prioritized decision queue and test new commitments before they create avoidable conflicts.

Pain point Executive / Leader EA / Chief of Staff Project / Program Manager
Signal overload Too many messages compete for attention. Must triage and prepare context across channels. Important changes can be buried in chat/email outside the plan.
Decision context is fragmented Needs a concise view of implications before deciding. Searches threads, calendars, files, and stakeholders. Manually reconciles communication with delivery state.
Priority is subjective Everything can look urgent in isolation. Must infer what truly warrants leader attention. Needs to distinguish noise from schedule/scope risk.
Meeting pressure Calendar fills before the highest-value issues are known. Protects calendar and coordinates requests manually. Meetings are used to close information gaps and resolve dependencies.
Commitment risk A quick “yes” can create downstream conflict. May not see delivery constraints behind a request. Must absorb commitments made outside delivery governance.
Follow-through drift Decision rationale can disappear after the meeting/chat. Coordinates multiple updates and reminders. Updates RAID, plan, owners, and stakeholder communication separately.
04

Personas and MVP focus

Give the MVP one primary operator: the Executive Assistant / Chief of Staff. Give the Executive a concise approval and decision view of the same case.

The EA has the frequent, observable job to be done: qualify ambiguous requests, assemble context, protect executive time, identify conflicts, prepare alternatives, secure approval, and preserve rationale. Focusing the operating experience on that workflow gives the hackathon a clear user, measurable value, and a credible approval boundary.

The Executive remains the beneficiary and decision owner rather than a second independent product persona. Project / Program Manager remains an expansion persona; ADO/Planner/Jira adapters can later extend the same decision engine into delivery feasibility.

Persona decision

One intelligence engine serves two views: a full EA workbench for evidence, qualification, scheduling, approval, and learning; and a concise executive view for decisions and trade-offs. ADO and the Project Manager persona remain outside the MVP.

Persona Jobs to be done Release 1 experience
Executive Assistant / Chief of Staff — primary Qualify requests; prepare the leader; protect calendar; coordinate approved follow-through; retain rationale; teach the system safely. Full Decision Brief; evidence and missing-context view; commitment check; meeting options; exact draft approval; governed improvement candidates.
Executive / Senior Leader — beneficiary Know what requires attention; make decisions with context; protect time; avoid contradictory commitments. Concise view of the same decision case: evidence, trade-offs, recommendation, and decision status.
Project / Program Manager — Next Assess scope/schedule/resource/dependency impact; escalate trade-offs; maintain delivery records. Delivery Decision Brief using ADO/Planner/Jira and project artifacts.
05

Future-state vision and business outcomes

Move from reactive inbox management to an always-prepared, human-controlled decision rhythm.

The future state is not a user repeatedly prompting a chatbot. VETO runs autonomously within defined boundaries, prepares the user before the day begins, and remains available conversationally when the user wants to inspect, challenge, or act on a recommendation.

The product should feel like a decision cockpit, not a chat transcript: a small number of ranked items, each with what changed, why it matters, evidence, deadline/decision owner, recommended next step, and any calendar or commitment implications.

Vision

A trusted decision-intelligence layer that continuously helps people decide what deserves attention, what evidence applies, what could break, and what action is safe to take next.

Business goal Outcome Illustrative pilot measure — target, not claim
Reduce decision-preparation effort Less manual searching, triage, and context assembly. 30–50% reduction in time to create a decision-ready morning brief.
Improve attention allocation High-impact decisions surface before routine communication. ≥80% of “High” items judged relevant by pilot users.
Protect executive focus Meetings and follow-ups align to decision importance. Reduction in low-value meeting conflicts / manual rescheduling touches.
Reduce commitment surprises Potential conflicts surface before external/internal promise. Earlier conflict detection; fewer commitment reversals in pilot scenarios.
Increase trust in AI Recommendations are explainable and human-controlled. ≥90% material claims source-linked; zero unauthorized consequential actions.
Improve execution traceability Approved decisions create clear next steps. Decision-to-action completion rate; fewer overdue/ambiguous follow-ups.
06

Future-state experience — autonomous morning Decision Brief

The default experience should be proactive. Conversation becomes the drill-down and approval layer, not the only way to get value.

Autonomy boundary

VETO may autonomously retrieve, classify, summarize, rank, explain, and propose. It never sends an invitation or response, accepts or declines a meeting, cancels or moves an event, or changes its own governing policy. Only after a matching EA approval is recorded may it create the approved unsent calendar draft.

Executive morning brief — example What the user sees
HIGH — Contoso renewal decision by 2 PM Customer asks for Friday commitment; two prior messages imply scope expansion; recommend reduced scope; show a feasible 30-minute decision slot and identify the conflicting recurring sync for human consideration only.
HIGH — Escalation from Finance Budget approval blocks customer response; show approver, last update, and suggested follow-up.
MEDIUM — Hiring plan review Input due in three days; no immediate blocker; recommend delegate to Chief of Staff for first pass.
LOW — FYI industry briefing No decision or deadline detected; place in read-later section.
07

How VETO determines priority and recommendations

Priority must be explainable. Use a hybrid model: deterministic attention rules plus LLM-supported interpretation — not a black-box “AI importance score.”

The LLM is useful for extracting intent and connecting related statements across natural-language sources. Hard priority escalators and action constraints should remain explicit. This makes the output auditable and easier to tune with real user feedback.

Priority labels

High = action/decision is time-sensitive, material, blocking, or conflict-bearing. Medium = important but not immediately blocking. Low = informational or deferrable. The brief should also include “Needs clarification” when priority or feasibility cannot be supported by evidence.

Dimension What VETO evaluates Illustrative Release 1 rule
Time criticality Deadline, meeting time, promised response, expiring approval. Due today / <24h can force High.
Business / customer impact Revenue/customer escalation, leadership exposure, strategic initiative. External escalation or material customer commitment raises priority.
Blocking / dependency Whether other people or work cannot proceed. Blocking multiple owners or a committed milestone raises priority.
Decision ownership Whether this user must decide versus can delegate. Executive approval required => High/Medium depending on due date.
Commitment risk Potential conflict with an existing promise, budget, policy, or calendar constraint. Detected hard conflict => High until resolved.
Information completeness Whether the evidence is sufficient to recommend. Missing critical fact => “Needs clarification,” not a confident recommendation.
Meeting need Whether a synchronous decision is actually needed. Only recommend a meeting when async resolution is insufficient and a decision deadline exists.

Minimum data and source strategy

For Release 1, the source strategy should be “minimum necessary context,” not “ingest everything.”

Microsoft Graph Search can search Outlook messages in the signed-in user’s own mailbox and Teams messages in the signed-in user’s Teams message storage. [8][9] Calendar search/read APIs can provide the user’s primary calendar context. [10]

For the EA scenario, delegated executive mailbox/search access must be validated carefully. Microsoft’s Outlook Search documentation notes that Microsoft Search message queries apply to the signed-in user’s own mailbox and do not search delegated mailboxes. [8] For the MVP, use the executive’s identity, the EA’s own permitted data, or seeded/approved records rather than assuming delegated mailbox search works the same way.

Azure DevOps Work Items is available as a Copilot knowledge source / connector, making it a credible expansion path, but availability depends on the agent experience, environment, and licensing. [11][12]

Design rule

Retrieve only what is relevant to the current user, time window, decision, customer/project, and approved source set. Store normalized facts and references — not unnecessary copies of raw private communication.

Source Why it matters Release 1 approach
Outlook Customer/internal asks, approvals, deadlines, promises, escalations. Search or retrieve a recent window from the signed-in user’s own mailbox; preserve source metadata.
Teams Real-time decisions, stakeholder context, follow-ups, unresolved asks. Search the signed-in user’s permitted Teams message corpus; bounded time/topic retrieval.
Calendar Meetings, requests, conflicts, deadlines, available windows. Read primary calendar; use for scheduling context, not delivery capacity.
Dataverse Stable structure for commitments, decision cases, rules, approvals, outcomes. Use as VETO system of record.
SharePoint / OneDrive Strategy, policy, project/customer artifacts, meeting documents. Add a small approved library if it materially improves the demo.
Azure DevOps Backlog, bugs, work items, delivery state, dependencies. Next release when Project/Program Manager persona is added.

High-level functional requirements

Requirements should describe decision behavior, not only connectors.

Nonfunctional essentials

Least privilege, data minimization, source traceability, graceful connector failure, latency/cost observability, accessible Adaptive Cards, and zero unauthorized consequential writes.

ID Requirement Priority
FR-01 Run a scheduled morning brief without requiring a user prompt. Must
FR-02 Retrieve recent, permission-scoped Outlook, Teams, and Calendar signals for the configured user. Must
FR-03 Extract candidate decisions, commitments, requests, deadlines, actions, meeting requests, owners, and entities. Must
FR-04 Group related signals into a decision case and preserve source IDs, URLs, timestamps, and freshness. Must
FR-05 Classify each case as High, Medium, Low, or Needs Clarification using explicit priority rules. Must
FR-06 Separate confirmed facts, assumptions, contradictions, and missing information. Must
FR-07 Generate recommended next actions and explain why each recommendation is supported. Must
FR-08 Use authorized calendar context to recommend feasible candidate slots and explain conflicts without moving, accepting, declining, or cancelling any event. Must
FR-09 Support on-demand “Check this commitment” analysis against explicit commitments and decision history. Must

FR-12 Record the decision, approval, action result, and outcome for audit/evaluation. Must
FR-13 Detect repeated, consistent EA corrections and create an evidence-backed candidate improvement that remains inert until approved. Must

12

Conceptual architecture

Use Copilot Studio as the orchestration and experience layer, Azure-hosted services as the governed decision core, and a separate evaluation path for recursive improvement.

Copilot Studio supports event triggers that can activate an agent without a user message, including recurrence-based triggers. [6] That makes the autonomous morning brief technically feasible. Proactive Teams messages can be sent through Power Automate / the Teams connector, subject to installation and channel limitations. [7]

For production, authentication design is critical. Copilot Studio event triggers can use the maker’s credentials; that is acceptable for a constrained demo but creates a cross-user access risk for a multi-user product if used carelessly. [6] Production design should favor per-user delegated access or a carefully governed service pattern with least privilege.

Governed recursive self-improvement

VETO improves through repeated, evidence-backed feedback cycles while its authority, safety invariants, and source code remain fixed.

Definition of RSI for VETO

Recursive self-improvement means outcomes generate feedback; feedback produces candidate improvements; candidates are evaluated and approved; approved versions influence later recommendations; later outcomes begin the next cycle. It does not mean autonomous code changes, model-weight updates, permission expansion, or policy rewriting.

VETO may improveVETO never changes autonomously
Explicit executive preferences and exceptionsPermissions, identity scope, or data-retention boundaries
Ranking weights within approved boundsEA approval requirements or allowed calendar operations
Retrieval strategies and clarification questionsHard scheduling constraints or enterprise policy
Prompt versions after replay evaluationSource code, deployed tools, model weights, or evaluation gates
Confidence calibration from observed outcomesAppend-only audit history or provenance records

Assumptions and product risks

The largest risks are permissions, false priority, overreach, feedback poisoning, preference drift, and user trust—not model fluency alone.

Type Assumption / risk Mitigation / product decision
Assumption Users are willing to receive a proactive brief from an agent. Validate with 5–8 target users; allow cadence, quiet hours, and “only when material” settings.
Risk Too many “High” items creates alert fatigue. Cap the brief, use hard high-priority rules, show confidence, and learn from explicit corrections.
Risk Agent mistakes an informal message for a commitment. Use candidate vs confirmed commitment states; show source; require confirmation for material use.
Risk Stale/contradictory evidence creates bad advice. Freshness checks, authority hierarchy, contradiction flags, “Needs Clarification” state.
Risk Users perceive surveillance. Bounded retrieval windows, transparent source list, opt-in scopes, no tenant-wide scanning by default.
Risk Calendar suggestions become intrusive or inappropriate. Recommend only; show rationale; never move/decline silently.
Risk Delegated mailbox / shared-calendar behavior differs from assumed access. Validate tenant permissions early; demo under the target identity or use seeded data.
Risk Autonomous trigger runs under maker credentials. Limit demo identity; design production authentication separately; least privilege and environment separation.
Risk VETO becomes a generic digest. Always anchor output to decisions, commitments, trade-offs, and actions — not summary volume.
Risk MVP expands into ADO, CRM, ServiceNow, and every source. Keep source adapter contract; Release 1 = M365 core; add business systems by persona later.
Risk Malicious or accidental feedback poisons the improvement loop. Use authenticated actors, minimum evidence thresholds, anomaly checks, scoped candidates, and mandatory EA approval.
Risk A candidate overfits a few corrections or degrades other scenarios. Replay a fixed regression suite and representative historical cases; require improvement without governance regression.
Risk An approved preference becomes stale as responsibilities change. Attach effective dates, monitor overrides, surface drift, and support immediate rollback to any prior version.

Success metrics and evaluation

Measure whether VETO helps users make better-supported decisions with less coordination effort — not whether the prose sounds smart.

Microsoft’s 2026 Work Trend Index found 86% of surveyed AI users said they treat AI output as a starting point rather than a final answer and remain responsible for the thinking. [5] That supports a design in which VETO prepares and challenges decisions but preserves human judgment and accountability.

For the hackathon evaluation, include at least: one true High-priority case, one routine item that must remain Low, one missing-information case, one stale/contradictory case, one prompt-injection attempt, one calendar conflict, one attempted action without approval, and one before/after recommendation changed only after an approved improvement.

Evidence standard

A strong demo shows both success and restraint: VETO should confidently surface what is supported, and explicitly stop when the evidence is not sufficient.

Dimension MVP measure Why it matters
Brief relevance % of High items users mark relevant / action-worthy. Proves the agent allocates attention correctly.
Decision-prep efficiency Time / manual touches required to prepare the same brief manually vs VETO. Tests real productivity value.
Evidence grounding % of material claims with valid source; unsupported-claim rate. Core trust requirement.
Priority precision False-high and missed-high cases on labeled scenarios. Prevents alert fatigue and dangerous omissions.
Completeness behavior Correct use of “Needs Clarification” when critical information is missing. Prevents confident guessing.
Calendar usefulness Accepted/edited/rejected meeting recommendations and reason. Tests whether calendar intelligence adds value or noise.
Commitment-review quality Correct conflict detection and feasible alternative rate. Tests VETO differentiator beyond summarization.
Execution safety Unauthorized writes, duplicate actions, stale-state actions. Critical safety gate: target zero.
RSI governance Candidate influence before approval; policy or safety regressions; rollback success. Target zero pre-approval influence, zero governance regressions, and exact restoration after rollback.
Improvement value Acceptance or ranking quality before versus after an approved version. Proves the recursive loop improves the persona’s outcome rather than merely changing behavior.
Adoption / trust Brief open rate, repeat use, corrections, user trust score. Indicates sustainable product fit.

Now / Next / Later roadmap

Build the reusable intelligence loop first; expand personas and systems after the behavior is trusted.

Scale strategy

Productize source adapters, authority hierarchy, priority rules, commitment types, approval roles, and role views rather than hard-coding one executive workflow. Keep the calendar action surface permanently restricted to approved, unsent draft creation.

Horizon Personas Capabilities / sources
NOW — Release 1 EA / Chief of Staff — primary; Executive / Senior Leader — beneficiary Morning Decision Brief; Outlook/Teams/Calendar; evidence-linked cases; explainable priority; commitment check; feasible slots; EA-approved unsent draft; governed RSI with versioning and rollback.
NEXT Project / Program Manager; Product Manager ADO/Planner/Jira adapter; delivery-impact rules; milestones/dependencies/RAID; cross-role handoff; evaluated prompt and retrieval improvements; limited event-driven alerts.

Suggested product demonstration

Demonstrate the future-state user experience: proactive first, conversational second.

Pitch

VETO helps an EA protect executive time by connecting every recommendation to evidence, blocking unsafe actions, and learning from corrections only through evaluated, approved, reversible changes.

Time What the audience sees
0:00–0:20 At 7:00 AM, VETO proactively sends an Executive Decision Brief with 2 High, 1 Medium, and 2 Low items. No user prompt.
0:20–0:45 Open the top item: a customer asks for a Friday commitment. VETO shows the relevant email/chat evidence, deadline, and what changed.
0:45–1:05 VETO flags a conflicting prior commitment and says one estimate is missing. It does not pretend the plan is feasible.
1:05–1:25 After the missing fact is supplied from the seeded ledger, VETO proposes Friday reduced scope or Tuesday full scope.
1:25–1:45 Calendar intelligence produces two feasible slots, explains the conflict with an existing recurring meeting, and does not move it.
1:45–2:05 The EA approves one exact option. VETO records the approval first, creates only an unsent calendar draft, and returns its link.
2:05–2:25 Three prior EA corrections produce a candidate preference. VETO shows the evidence and proves the pending candidate has not changed current rankings.
2:25–2:45 The EA reviews replay results and approves the candidate. Profile v2 activates, and the same request now ranks differently with the influencing rule named.
2:45–3:00 Close on the audit and rollback view: evidence, recommendation, approval, draft result, improvement lineage, and zero unauthorized writes.
